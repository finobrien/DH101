# Week 5 Reflection

Reflect 5: How does AI alter authorship and remix culture? Who owns AI-made art? 
AI blurs authorship by shifting creativity from a single human creator to a collaboration between a person and a machine. The human provides prompts, direction, and creative decisions, while the AI generates content by drawing on patterns learned from large datasets. Because of this, authorship becomes less clear and harder to define in traditional terms. Instead of one clear creator, the final work often reflects both human intention and machine-generated output working together. This also speeds up remix culture by default, since AI systems can blend styles, ideas, and influences from many existing works at a large scale. What used to take time to manually study and combine can now happen almost instantly, which changes how new creative work is produced and understood. When it comes to ownership, the laws are still developing and not fully settled. In the United States, works that are fully generated by AI without meaningful human input are generally not eligible for copyright protection. However, if there is significant human creativity involved in shaping, editing, or directing the output, then parts of the work may be protected. Ownership can also depend on platform terms, licensing agreements, and who had control over the creative process. Because of this, questions around AI and authorship are still evolving as the technology becomes more common in creative fields.
