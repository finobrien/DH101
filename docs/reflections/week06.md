# Week 6 Reflection

Reflect 6: What do we gain/lose when machines “read” literature for us? 
When machines read literature for us, we gain a lot of efficiency and the ability to see large-scale patterns across huge amounts of text that would be difficult to notice on our own. Ideas like “distant reading,” developed by Franco Moretti, show how machines can track themes, word patterns, and literary trends across hundreds or even thousands of novels in just seconds. This can be incredibly powerful because it allows us to study literature from a broader, more data-driven perspective. At the same time, something important is lost in this process. While machines can identify patterns in a novel like Beloved or other texts, they cannot actually experience the emotional depth or personal impact of the story. A machine can recognize themes like trauma, memory, or loss, but it doesn’t feel the weight of those ideas in the way a human reader does. That emotional response is a key part of what makes literature meaningful. Literature is not just data or patterns; it is also interpretation, emotion, and lived experience. Reading is often a personal process where meaning is shaped by the reader’s own background and feelings. Because of that, machine analysis and human reading offer two very different kinds of understanding. One gives scale and structure, while the other gives depth and emotional connection.
