# Week 8 Reflection

Reflect 8: Who is visible/invisible in AI networks? How does visualization reveal or obscure power? 
In AI networks, some people and organizations are often very visible, such as big tech companies, well-funded research labs, and well-known researchers. At the same time, smaller groups, independent researchers, and communities that are affected by AI systems are often much less visible or even completely absent from view. This creates an uneven picture of who is actually involved in building and shaping AI. Visualizations of these networks can help show how power and influence are distributed, especially by highlighting which actors are central or highly connected. In that sense, they can make complex systems easier to understand and can even help reveal patterns of collaboration or control. However, they can also hide important parts of the picture depending on what data is included. If a visualization only uses certain kinds of information, like funding sources, citations, or formal partnerships, it may unintentionally leave out informal contributors, laborers, or impacted communities. As a result, the way a network is mapped is never completely neutral. It depends on the choices made by the person creating it and the data they decide to include or exclude. Because of this, network visualizations can sometimes make certain actors seem more important or central than they actually are in practice, while downplaying or erasing others. Understanding this limitation is important when interpreting AI networks, since what appears on the map is shaped as much by design decisions as it is by reality.
