# Week 3 Reflection

Reflect 3: What does “authenticity” mean when identity is co-constructed with AI? 
When identity is co-constructed with AI, authenticity doesn’t really mean being completely original or “100% real.” Instead, it becomes more about how much control and intention I have in the process. The identity or image that AI creates isn’t fully me, but it also isn’t totally separate from me either. It’s shaped by the prompts I give, the choices I make, and also by the AI itself, with its limitations, biases, and the patterns it has learned from data. Because of this, what gets created is always influenced by both sides. This changes how I think about authenticity. It’s less about whether something is perfectly accurate and more about being aware of how AI is shaping the outcome. AI decides, in a way, what gets emphasized and what gets left out, and that affects how identity is presented. If I don’t think about that, it’s easy to assume the result is a direct reflection of who I am, when it’s actually more complicated than that. There’s always some level of interpretation happening through the system. What feels more authentic, then, is recognizing that the result is a collaboration rather than a pure reflection of myself. It’s about understanding my role in guiding the process while also acknowledging the influence of the technology. That awareness makes the final outcome feel more honest, even if it isn’t perfectly “real.”
