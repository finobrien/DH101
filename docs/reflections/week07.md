# Week 7 Reflection

Reflect 7: How does AI reshape global geographies of power, labor, and environment? AI reshapes global geographies of power, labor, and the environment by concentrating control in a few powerful places while distributing the costs across the world. Most advanced AI systems are developed by major companies like OpenAI and Google, which are primarily based in the United States, alongside competition from countries such as China. This concentration of development and infrastructure gives a small number of organizations and regions significant influence over how AI is designed, deployed, and regulated, which can reinforce existing global inequalities. At the same time, much of the less visible labor that supports AI systems is outsourced to other parts of the world. Tasks like data labeling, content moderation, and training support are often carried out in countries such as Kenya and India, where workers may be paid relatively low wages and work under demanding conditions. This means that while AI appears highly automated, it still depends heavily on human labor that is often hidden from view. AI also has environmental consequences that are unevenly distributed. Large-scale data centers require significant energy and water resources, and the hardware that powers AI depends on minerals extracted from places such as the Democratic Republic of the Congo. As a result, environmental costs are frequently shifted away from the regions that benefit most from AI development. Overall, AI tends to concentrate benefits, infrastructure, and decision-making power in a small number of global centers, while distributing labor demands and environmental impacts across a much wider and more unequal global network.
