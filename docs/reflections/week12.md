# Week 12 Reflection

AI is not inherently sustainable or unsustainable; it depends on how it is designed, built, and powered. AI systems require large amounts of electricity, data centers, and hardware, all of which can increase carbon emissions and resource use. This impact becomes especially significant when the energy powering these systems comes from fossil fuels. The production of hardware also relies on raw materials and global supply chains, which adds to the environmental footprint. At the same time, AI also has the potential to support more sustainable practices. It can help improve energy efficiency in buildings and transportation, optimize industrial processes, and assist in predicting climate patterns and environmental changes. These applications show how AI can be used as a tool for addressing environmental challenges rather than only contributing to them. Because of this, the ecological impact of AI is not straightforward. It involves a trade-off between the costs of running and maintaining these systems and the potential benefits they provide in terms of optimization and innovation. A key question is whether the advantages gained from AI outweigh its growing energy and material demands. Ultimately, the sustainability of AI depends on how its infrastructure is developed in the future. Shifting toward cleaner energy sources, more efficient systems, and responsible design choices will play a major role in determining whether AI becomes part of the environmental problem or part of the solution.
