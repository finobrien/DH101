Final Reflection

Over the semester, I learned to see digital humanities less as a set of tools and more as a way of asking better questions about culture, power, and evidence. I got comfortable with close reading alongside data and visualization, practiced ethical thinking about AI and labor, and built skills in structuring arguments through making. The most interesting part was how every project forced me to connect technical choices to human consequences, while the most challenging part was sitting with uncertainty—there isn’t always a clean answer, but there is a clearer responsibility.

What does it mean to be human in the age of AI?

Being human in the age of AI means understanding both what AI can do and what it can’t. AI is able to generate language, recognize patterns, and even create things that seem creative, but it doesn’t have real experiences, emotions, or true understanding. It can copy how humans think and communicate, but it doesn’t actually feel or live through anything. Throughout this course, I’ve started to see AI more as a tool than a replacement for people. It can help make things faster and more efficient, but it still depends on human ideas and decisions. This made me realize that being human isn’t just about intelligence, especially now that AI can do a lot of thinking tasks. What really matters more is things like emotion, connection, and the ability to give meaning to experiences. The debate about humans vs AI usually focuses on what AI can do, but I think it’s more important to look at what it can’t do. AI can’t build real relationships, feel emotions, or make moral decisions based on real-life experiences. Humans are shaped by what they go through, and that’s what gives our actions meaning. One of the biggest things I learned in this class is that being human isn’t just about thinking, it’s about empathy, self-awareness, and reflection. AI can imitate those things, but it doesn’t actually have them. In the end, being human in the age of AI means knowing that technology can help us, but it doesn’t define us, and what really makes us human is our ability to feel, connect, and make meaning out of our lives.
