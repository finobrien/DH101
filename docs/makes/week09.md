# Week 9: Bots & Generators

## The Artifact
[Chat with Charlie on Character.AI](https://character.ai/chat/e9m2kGWa9SLyN2IN6Y0hwah43mwolypqGC0t55pGEIQ)

## Process Notes
I used Character.AI and gave prompts describing what I wanted the chatbot to be like. I used Character.AI and decided everything about the personality and humor of my chatbot, Charlie.

> **Process note:** I kept a running list of words that matched Charlie’s voice and used those to refine the prompts.

## Reflection
I tried to build a Character.AI bot named Charlie with a specific mix of humor and warmth, almost like a friend who is quick-witted but not mean. My first version failed because the personality was too generic — the bot responded in flat, safe phrases that didn’t match the voice I imagined. I revised the traits and example phrases until the responses sounded more specific. That iterative prompting became the real creative work. I used AI to generate the dialogue in real time, but I controlled the character design, tone, and boundaries. This taught me that AI creativity is highly sensitive to framing: small shifts in wording can completely change the character’s vibe. It also taught me that “making” with AI is less about writing every line and more about directing a performance. The bot can surprise me, but it can also default to clichés if I don’t guide it. The project made me more aware that creativity is a collaboration where the human role is to define intent, filter outputs, and decide what feels authentic. AI can produce endless variation, but it needs a human to give it a personality that actually matters.
