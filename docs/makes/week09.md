## Reflection
Creativity changed when AI became capable of generating endless responses, because it shifted the focus from purely coming up with ideas to selecting, shaping, and refining the best ones. Instead of starting from nothing, the creative process often begins with a wide range of possible outputs, and the challenge becomes deciding which direction to take and how to develop it further. In that sense, creativity is less about producing every idea from scratch and more about curating and guiding what emerges. The human role is still essential in this process, because we provide the prompts, set the direction, and judge which outputs are meaningful or useful. AI can generate possibilities, but it does not fully understand intent in the same way a person does, so human decision-making is what gives those outputs purpose. When I built my bot, I noticed that even small changes in wording, tone, or personality traits could significantly affect the responses it produced. This made the process feel more like an interaction or back-and-forth exchange rather than a one-way tool. I had influence over the output, but not complete control, since the AI could still surprise me or sometimes produce repetitive or unexpected responses. Overall, the AI was responsible for generating a wide set of possibilities, but I was responsible for selecting, shaping, and assigning meaning to them. That balance is what made the process feel creative rather than fully automated.
