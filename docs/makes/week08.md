# Week 8: Networks of Knowledge & Power

## The Artifact
![Alt text: network chain sketch translated into a digital image](../assets/images/week08.png)

*Network chain sketch translated into a digital image.*

## Process Notes
I identified the sources, targets, relationship types, and node types, then connected them into a “chain.” After sketching the idea on paper, I sent it to AI to turn it into an image for my project. I used pen and paper, ChatGPT, and several corporate websites, and I decided which sources to include, the structure, and what appeared in the final network.

## Reflection
I tried to turn a hand-drawn network chain into a digital visualization that made power relations in AI visible. My first attempt failed because the AI-generated image rearranged relationships and simplified some of the nodes I wanted to keep distinct. It looked clean, but it blurred the difference between sources of funding, research labs, and affected communities. To fix that, I tightened my prompt and kept the sketch as a reference while I checked the output against my original structure. I used AI for rendering the final image, but the conceptual map — the sources, targets, and relationship types — was entirely mine. This project taught me that AI can translate style, but it can also distort meaning if I don’t supervise it closely. What I learned about AI and creativity is that creativity here is the architecture: deciding which connections matter and which voices get represented. The AI made the image faster, but it didn’t make the argument. That still came from my choices about what to include, what to leave out, and how to show the network’s imbalance. The final image is a collaboration, but the responsibility for its meaning stayed with me.
