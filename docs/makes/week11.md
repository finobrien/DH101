## Reflection
The invisible work of AI is done by a wide range of people who are often overlooked, including data labelers, content moderators, low-paid contractors, and everyday users whose data is collected and used to train these systems. These workers help clean, sort, and filter the data that makes AI function, but they usually receive very little recognition for their contributions. Even though their labor is essential to building and maintaining AI systems, it often remains hidden behind the idea that AI is fully automated. At the same time, the biggest profits tend to go to large tech companies that develop and own AI systems, as well as to investors and organizations that use AI to increase efficiency, reduce costs, and scale operations. This creates a clear imbalance in how benefits are distributed across the system. Those doing essential behind-the-scenes labor often gain the least, while those who control the technology and its infrastructure gain the most economic and institutional power. As a result, AI development is not just about algorithms or technical innovation, but also about global systems of labor and value that are unevenly structured. Much of the work that makes AI possible is distributed across different countries and economic contexts, but the rewards are concentrated in a much smaller number of places and institutions.
