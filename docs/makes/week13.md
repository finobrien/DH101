## Reflection
AI futures are most realistically somewhere between utopian and dystopian rather than fully one or the other. While AI has the potential to improve areas like healthcare, education, and everyday life, it also brings serious challenges such as job displacement, bias in decision-making systems, and unequal access to technology. Because of this, most realistic predictions about AI include both positive and negative outcomes happening at the same time, rather than a single clear direction. Utopian visions of AI often reflect our hopes for progress, fairness, and technological solutions to complex social problems. They imagine AI as a tool that can reduce inequality, improve efficiency, and make life easier for people. On the other hand, dystopian visions tend to focus on fears about losing control, increased surveillance, or the reinforcement of existing inequalities through automated systems. These concerns highlight what could go wrong if AI is developed or used without careful oversight. Overall, these different visions reveal more about human values and concerns than about the technology itself. They show how we project our hopes, fears, and assumptions onto AI as it becomes more present in everyday life. In that sense, AI is not just a technological development, but also a social and cultural one. The way we imagine its future reflects our current priorities and anxieties, and ultimately says a lot about how we choose to understand progress and control in the present.
